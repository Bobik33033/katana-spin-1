How the peg board is set up

The board is a triangle made of staggered pegs. Each row is wider than the one above it, so the ball has more possible paths as it falls. At every peg it has two basic directions to travel, left or right, and that pattern repeats until it reaches the bottom pockets.

Because each bounce is a fresh split in the path, most balls end up near the middle rather than at the far edges. That is why casino katana spin results often follow a familiar curve: central pockets appear more often, while outer pockets can pay more because they land less often.

Six words worth knowing

Pocket
The slot at the bottom of the board where a finished ball lands and sets the payout.
Rows
The number of peg lines crossed by the ball; more rows mean more pockets and a sharper curve.
Risk level
A preset that changes how much value sits in the middle versus the edges.
House edge
The built-in margin that keeps the game profitable for the operator over time.
Provably fair
A system that lets players verify the round outcome after the ball has dropped.
Variance
The amount of swing around the average result, which is what the risk setting mostly controls.
